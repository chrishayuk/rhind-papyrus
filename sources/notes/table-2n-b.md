# Notes: table of 2 ÷ n, entries 2 ÷ 51 to 2 ÷ 101; table of 1-9 ÷ 10

Files: `papyrus/table-2n-b.rmp` (26 entries), `papyrus/table-n10.rmp` (9 entries).

Chace vol. II, PDF pp. 77-114: literal translation on the odd page, plate on the even page (plates 15-33).
2 ÷ 51 is on PDF p. 77 (with 2 ÷ 49), so the range starts there, not at p. 79.
Peet 1923, printed pp. 43-47 (PDF pp. 47-51) for 2 ÷ 51 to 2 ÷ 101; p. 50 (PDF p. 54) for the table of tenths.
PDF page = printed page + 4 throughout this range.

All reading was done by a coding agent's sub-agent (one agent for this range). No person has checked it.

## How it was read

- Chace's translation pages were rendered at 200 dpi, and every entry was cropped and enlarged (several at 400 dpi:
  2 ÷ 71, 89, 91, 93, 95, 97, 99, 101 and the table of tenths). Figures, dots, ⅔ signs, brackets, ticks and red ink were
  read off the images; the OCR was not used for figures.
- Chace's plates were looked at for 2 ÷ 53/55 (pl. 16), 57/59 (pl. 17), 85/87 (pl. 29) and 101 / tenths (pl. 33), and
  the plate glosses were read closely for the red '226 of 2 ÷ 59 (below). For the other entries the translation page,
  which prints red in red, was taken as Chace's reading; those plates were not read glyph by glyph.
- Peet's pages were rendered at 200 dpi and read whole.

## Conventions (what the notation could not hold directly)

- **Answer line.** In the papyrus each entry's answer is one line: red part of n, then in black what it gives. Written
  one pair to a row (`{'30}  1 2/3 '10`), in the papyrus's order.
- **Divisor at the head.** The divisor stands alone in black at the head of most entries. Written as a quoted line
  (`"57"`), as in `table-2n-a.rmp`. In 2 ÷ 87 the head has `1 87`, and in 2 ÷ 85 the working has a row `1 85`; these
  are rows of the papyrus and are written as rows.
- **Side reckonings (three figures).** Rows such as `\ 4  '236  '4` (2 ÷ 59) are the scribe's compressed form: the
  multiplier k at the left, ticked, then k × n (dotted or not), then the part of n that 1/(kn) gives. Chace (note to
  2 ÷ 17): "in most of the multiplications carried through for the purpose of obtaining fractional multipliers that
  will produce given fractions the partial products are written without dots. See 2 divided by 23, 37, 41, and 53."
  Written in the papyrus's order. A checker that reads the first two figures as multiplier and product of n will be
  right for the undotted middles (2/3 × 57 = 38, 2 × 57 = 114) and wrong for the dotted ones ('236); the third figure
  is what a checker should test (n × 1/(kn)). The caller's "left = unit fraction, right = what it gives" holds for the
  two-figure rows only.
- **Undotted parts.** Where Chace prints a part without its dot (the ticked `\ 30` of 2 ÷ 53, `\ 40` of 61 and 67,
  `\ 56` of 97, `60` of 89 and 95, `42`, `46`, `50`, `58`, `66` in the ⅔ rows), it is written as a bare number, as
  printed. These read as whole numbers to `rhind`; the value meant is the part.
- **"Find" (gm)** is written as a quoted line before its row; **"Working-out:" (šsm·t)** as a red quoted line;
  **"Call 2 out of n"** as a red line `{"Call 2"}` and a black line `"out of n"`.
- **"Remainder" (ḏꜣ·t) '15** in 2 ÷ 53 is written as the statement `Remainder '15.` (2 less 1 2/3 '10 less '6).
- **"Total" (dmd) 15** in 2 ÷ 53 stands at the head of the row `15  '795  '15` and totals the multipliers (10 + 5),
  not the products, so it is written as the quoted word `"Total"` before that row, not as a `Total` statement.
- **Partial restorations** inside a figure are written as Chace prints them: `'3[08]` (77), `{'77[6]}` (97),
  `{['53]4}` (89), `{'3[03]}` (101), `[9]3` (93).
- **Table of tenths.** The papyrus has no dividends, only the values, in two columns (1-6, then 7-9, side by side with
  rows 1-3). The headings `n ÷ 10` were added as asked; the pairing of rows is said in the file's header note.

## Entry by entry

Confident unless said otherwise. "Peet dot" differences are listed at the end, not here.

- **2 ÷ 51.** Read with confidence. Chace restores the black '2 after '102 as damage: `['2]`. Peet prints it in
  pointed brackets, ⟨'2⟩, which in his list of conventional signs "enclose words or passages which never stood in the papyrus,
  but whose omission there is due to an error on the part of the scribe". **Disagreement** (damage vs scribal omission).
- **2 ÷ 53.** Confident. Chace prints the side-column 53 dotted, with the note "In this multiplication we should expect
  dots with all the numbers of the second column, or with only the last, or not at all (see note to 2 divided by 17)."
  Peet: "There is a dot (= multiplier 1) after 'found' which should not be there. Cf. 2 ÷ 29 and 2 ÷ 31." (scribal
  error noted by Peet; Chace prints no such dot).
- **2 ÷ 55.** Confident.
- **2 ÷ 57.** Confident. Chace on the red 38 of the answer: "Fractional dot omitted."
- **2 ÷ 59.** The red figure of the answer line is **'226** in Chace's translation, and the plate's hieratic and gloss
  have 226 (two hundreds, two tens, six); the working has '236 (= 4 × 59). Chace makes no comment. Peet prints 236 with
  no remark. **Disagreement**, and a scribal error that neither edition points out. Read with confidence (checked at
  400 dpi on the plate).
- **2 ÷ 61.** Confident.

## Table of 1-9 ÷ 10

Read with confidence (400 dpi). Values: '10; '5; '5 '10; '3 '15; '2; '2 '10; 2/3 '30; 2/3 '10 '30; 2/3 '5 30.
All black. The last 30 (9 ÷ 10) is undotted. Chace, note 4: "The '30 in line 1 is correctly reproduced on Peet's
Plate E but appears as restored on his Plate F, and in his text, page 50. The same fraction in line 3 appears correctly
on Peet's Plate F, where he has restored the fractional dot omitted by the scribe, and in his text, but his Plate E
shows '10." So: **scribal error** (dot omitted on the 30 of 9 ÷ 10, Chace), and **disagreements**: Peet's text has
2/3 ['30] for 7 ÷ 10 (restored), and 2/3 '5 '30 (dotted) for 9 ÷ 10. Peet says (p. 50) that the table comes from New
York fragment 6 and that the values for 3/10, 4/10 and 5/10 are not proved in the problems that follow.

## Peet's dots (normalisations)

Peet, p. 38: "the incorrect omission or insertion of the fractional dot ... has been corrected without further comment
in these division sums". So where Chace prints a bare number and Peet a part, it is Peet's correction, not another
reading. Places in the working where they differ (Chace bare, Peet dotted, unless said): 53: 30, and the side column's
53 (Chace dotted, Peet bare); 59: 531; 61: 40; 63: 42; 67: 40, 536; 69: 46, 138; 71: 568, 710; 73: 365, 5; 75: 50;
81: 162; 83: 332; 85: 51, 255; 87: 58; 89: 60; 91: 30; 93: 166/186; 95: 60; 97: 56, 679, 776; 99: 66, 198. Peet's
answer lines are prose ("1 '2 is '34th") and every part in them is a fraction, so the red undotted figures of the
answers (38, 42, 40, 46, 54, 58, 380) were not compared.

## Scribal errors the editions point out

- 2 ÷ 51: '2 after '102 omitted (Peet, pointed brackets; Chace treats it as damage).
- 2 ÷ 53: a dot (= multiplier 1) after "found" "which should not be there" (Peet).
- 2 ÷ 57, 81, 87: fractional dot omitted on the red 38, 54, 58 (Chace).
- 2 ÷ 63, 67, 69, 71: fractional dot omitted with the sign for 40 (red 42, 40, 46, 40; Chace, note to 2 ÷ 21).
- 2 ÷ 79: '233 and '416 for '237 and '316 (both editions).
- 2 ÷ 83: 63 written for 83 (Chace).
- 2 ÷ 91: fractional dot omitted on the 30 of 2/3 '30, answer and working (Chace).
- 2 ÷ 93: 166 written for 186, answer and working (Chace).
- 9 ÷ 10: fractional dot omitted on the 30 (Chace, note 4).
- Not pointed out by either edition: 2 ÷ 59, red '226 in the answer line where the working has '236.
- Peet, p. 38, generally: "endless scribe's errors, the most usual being the omission of the tick ... and the incorrect
  omission or insertion of the fractional dot". In this range the unticked rows are 65 ('39), 71 ('40), 77 ('44),
  89 (60), 91 ('70 in Chace; Peet ticks it), 95 (60), 99 (2).
