What this is

The Rhind Mathematical Papyrus was copied by the scribe Ahmose around 1550 BCE from an older original. It opens with a table of 2 divided by every odd number from 3 to 101, written as sums of unit fractions, and goes on to 87 problems: loaves shared among men, quantities and their parts, granaries, fields, pyramids. The working is set out in columns of doublings, with a tick against the rows to be added.

Here each problem is transcribed from two editions, Chace (1929) and Peet (1923), in a notation that keeps the papyrus's layout, and run. rhind has no division: it divides as the scribe did, by doubling the divisor; it doubles an odd unit fraction only through the papyrus's own table. Each wrong figure is matched against models of slips: a hieratic sign written for another, the sign for "part" left off, a fraction dropped, the eye slipping a line.

Everything was read by a coding agent and its sub-agents, not by a person, and nothing is claimed as new until it has been checked against the photographs.

rhind is open source, Apache 2.0. Built with no dependencies and compiled for WASI; the editor is CodeMirror, and its colours, problems and hover help come from rhind's own language server running in the page.
