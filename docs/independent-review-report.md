## Findings

1. **High — “The commonest slip … half of everything that goes wrong” contradicts the primary edition.** README:147–149 treats all omitted part signs as errors. Chace vol. II printed p. xi (C10, upper right) says a dot is often omitted because context makes the fraction clear and expressly says its omission need not be an error; he cites Sethe 1916, p.87 n.1. C53, note to 2÷9, already estimates about ninety undotted fractions. C61 explains that the hieratic 40-sign already contains a dot and names the repeated omissions in 35,43,63,67,69,71 and Problems 32,33,61. C57/C73 describe optional dotting in compressed double statements. Conversely Peet P38 calls incorrect omission/insertion common scribal errors and says he corrected them without further comment. These are different editorial judgments, not unanimous support for the README's framing. Moreover the 71 includes an **added** dot (2÷41), while the omitted dot in No.65 is classified under `in-the-list`. **Change:** call this category “part-sign notation discrepancies”; distinguish contextual omission, damage/uncertainty, added signs and arithmetic mistakes. Suggested replacement: “Most flagged discrepancies involve fraction signs. Chace cautions that contextually clear omissions need not be errors.” Do not use 71/133 as a historical error rate.

2. **High — The 87/46 edition split is a keyword-search artifact, with demonstrably false negatives and false positives.** `src/papyrus.rs:35` searches for substrings on the figure's own note or a model-named line; it does not represent edition evidence. At table-2n-a:216 the actual note quotes Chace's explicit complaint about the added dot, but lacks a trigger word. At :232 it quotes the explicit 60/80 diagnosis, likewise missed. C61/C73 comments cover :231/:236/:237. No.30:8/:14, No.31:10/:20, No.32:8/:15/:30, No.37:17/:53 and No.38:17 have the omitted-dot footnote cited directly in their own comments, yet are called unnoted. At No.53:11 Peet's prose explicitly diagnoses inaccurate ticking. In the opposite direction, `errata` calls “no person checked it” and an error in Peet's own plate edition-error evidence. **Change:** replace prose substring matching with explicit structured source references and their scope: figure, multiple occurrences, convention, physical uncertainty, edition misprint, restoration. Withdraw the 46 as a source-based count until that evidence is curated. The ledger establishes the problem without an exhaustive search of every sentence of either book.

3. **High — Base voting assigns errors to correct figures and fails on ancient corrections.** `src/check.rs:201–264` maximizes agreement without reliable textual-state or confidence information. On the actual corpus it selects base **2/9** in No.11, **9** in No.12, **18** in No.14, **6** in No.18, and **42/97** in No.33's remainder division ([detail.txt](detail.txt)). Thus the original seventh in No.11 becomes wrong, corrected twenty-eighth in No.12 becomes wrong, correct No.18 eighteenth and total third become wrong, and No.33's correct twenty-first becomes a fantastically complicated expected value. C127 and Peet P55–56 explicitly explain the ancient corrections; the files flatten original and corrected readings into cells, and the checker ignores the correction cell outside table entries. The tie rule is not historical evidence. **Change:** encode original and amended states explicitly, select a documented base per column/state, and report ambiguity when inference is underdetermined. Recompute the census after this fix. Remove the `decisions` claim that No.12 is reduced to one wrong figure: the shipped result reports three wrong lines.

4. **High — The “carried” test is wrong for multiplier slips, and 35/38 does not establish pen slips.** `src/check.rs:267–273,430–502,603–622` repairs the *description* of an undotted multiplier but keeps its literal integer in `true_l`/`true_r`. No.22 writes 5 for a fifth, product 6, and total 9 = 3+6. The checker calls the total **153**, calls 9 wrong, and says the error was carried. No.37 similarly calls the correct 90 = 80+10 wrong against **10320**. These are exactly all three “carried” cases behind 35/38 ([audit.txt](audit.txt)). Putting back just these multiplier dots in memory removes the total errors ([detail.txt](detail.txt)); no repository edit is involved. `check_claim` also marks every wrong row “not carried” whenever a later total matches the inferred expected sum, even if the row is unticked or compensating errors make that inference impossible. A correct final answer alone does not prove the arithmetic was performed correctly. **Change:** distinguish literal notation, interpreted multiplier and expected product throughout dependency propagation; test each row's contribution independently; label propagation unknown when not identifiable. Withdraw the causal conclusion and rerun the tally.

5. **High — Restored statements are counted against the scribe despite the explicit contract.** `checkable()` at `src/check.rs:106` does not exclude `restored`; row checking handles that separately, but `check_claim():651` does not. Actual No.2 `Total [2]` is one of the purportedly unnoted scribal errors; both scans restore it. Probe `adversarial/restored-total.rmp` reports `Total [11]` as a wrong papyrus figure. No.61:34 contains restored `'54` yet is counted wrong. Lists also let the slip model propose changes to restored list members. **Change:** propagate restoration provenance to totals/results/list diagnoses, return Unchecked for restored claims, and distinguish testing an edition's restoration from testing a surviving figure. Add regression cases for both agreeing and disagreeing restored statements.

6. **High — Partial divisions, remainders and completed answers are attached to the wrong quantities.** No.70 explicitly stops at 99¾, writes remainder ¼, supplies the remaining fractional multiplier, then gives pefsu `12 2/3 '42 '126`. All agree with both editions. The checker raises three unnoted findings: “ticked rows make 99¾, not 100”, “remainder is 12⅔”, and “result is ¼”. Outside the table, `Statement::Remainder` at `src/check.rs:899` reuses pending result candidates instead of subtracting the accumulated products from the target; later `Result` uses the immediately preceding little column rather than the completed reckoning. With no ticks it supplies a quotient of **0**, falsely rejecting No.55's 3/5 and No.66's 3200/365. **Change:** model partial reckoning, subtotal, residual and continuation explicitly; infer no zero quotient from absent ticks. Treat selection as unknown or resolve it using a separately documented rule. Use Nos.55,66,70 as regression cases with exact expected quantities.

7. **High — Unit checking accepts incompatible dimensions and misadds convertible lists.** `src/check.rs:184` returns the value unchanged for every unrecognized unit pair. A row `1  1 hekat / 2  2 cubit` is marked entirely right. A list `1 hekat / 1 cubit / Total 2 hekat.` is right. Conversely `1 hekat / 320 ro / Total 2 hekat.` is rejected against **321 hekat**, because the list sum at :808 adds raw values before conversion. The single-figure parser does correctly support hekat/ro and setat/cubit-strip mixtures; the No.55 cubit-strip probe passes. That does not validate conversions between rows or list members. **Change:** centralize checked unit conversion, reject/mark unchecked incompatible dimensions, convert every list member into one compatible unit, and preserve the distinction between linear cubits and land strips. Correct docs/notation.md's promise that sums are checked only where units agree.

8. **Medium — “Relative” row acceptance can certify a wrong explicit multiplication.** The risk is acknowledged in `decisions`, but the output still gives an unconditional check mark. Probe `Multiply 10 by '2.` with rows `1 10`, `2 20`, ticked `'2 10`, `Result 10.` reports **four right, zero wrong**. The stated multiplication is 10×½=5. `src/check.rs:401` silently accepts half of the previous product; its ticks still count the multiplier as ½. In No.42, the opposite problem occurs: half of the *written* ten-row is rejected because `prev_true` holds the recomputed row instead. **Change:** represent a relative row's reference explicitly and use its effective multiplier in totals/ticks. Do not silently change an explicitly stated operation to make a row fit. Keep separate local-consistency and corrected-dependency checks.

9. **High — The core methodological claim exceeds what the checker implements.** README:32–39 says rhind has no other way to double an odd part and that a disagreement is therefore in the figure, not the method. Ordinary row expectations in `src/check.rs:395` are exact rational `l * b`, not a trace of `scribe::double`/halving/table operations. `src/scribe.rs:200` explicitly falls back to general `express(2/n)` when the table lacks an entry; `rhind double '103` successfully prints `'52 '5356`. `part_of():258` divides rational values directly; expression search reconstructs a representation. `src/table.rs` uses a corrected table at 43,59,93, not the literal transcribed answers, as `verify` itself reports. Exact value checking is useful, but it does not establish the scribe's method. **Change:** describe the checker as exact arithmetic under explicit interpretive assumptions, separate it from the reconstructed route generator, document the corrected table and fallback, or implement the claimed operational trace. Remove “the difference is in the figure, not in the method.”

10. **Medium — “133 wrong figures” is not a count of distinct wrong written figures.** In the table's three-cell branch (`src/check.rs:300–370`) a single corrupt middle denominator produces one multiplication failure and another reciprocal-product failure: table-2n-a:237 reports the same 66 twice; table-2n-b:175/:176/:250 behave likewise. The third half in `2 166 '2` is correct for the intended 186; the failed relation is not independent evidence of another wrong glyph. Conversely :231 combines missing signs on **two** different figures into one finding, and tick-target failures have no written figure span at all. **Change:** assign stable figure IDs, attach failed equations and suspected root causes to them, separate notation/selection/equation counts, and count a written figure once. State 133 as “diagnostic records under current rules” until this is fixed.

11. **Medium — 47/65 is reproducible, but 65 is a selected denominator, not every fully written column.** The read-only harness finds **141** problem columns with at least two rows, **130** `clean`, only **65** admitted by `routes::rhind_rows` (positive inferred base, first multiplier exactly 1, at least one tick). Fully surviving unticked columns and columns beginning with fractions are excluded, including Nos.7,16,26's proof,55,79,80. `judge()` at `src/routes.rs:75` compares multiplier values and ticks, not product figures or their Egyptian fraction forms. Targets are often derived from the same observed ticks being evaluated. Thus 40 exact multiplier/tick matches, +3 tens, +2 direct-third, +2 division =47 is a fit on this subset, not independent validation of the full procedure; error cases such as No.27 can still match the route. The remaining categories are heuristics, e.g. “Tens” only checks presence of 10. **Change:** disclose inclusion rules and exclusion counts beside the table; say “47 of 65 eligible columns match multiplier sequences and ticks after trying these conventions.” Avoid “the rest are where the scribe chose differently”: transcription and model errors also affect them.

12. **Medium — The 2÷n scores describe bounded, tuned searches against normalized targets.** Reproduced 4,25,29,32,34 of49 and the two jointly unexplained entries 13,95. However `rules.rs:110` limits general candidates to four parts and denominators ≤1000; divisor candidates additionally constrain `n/2 < m ≤ n`, distinct integer divisors, at most three residual terms, and the same denominator cap (:149–176). README's rule labels omit these conditions. A concrete consequence: for n=101 the “fewest parts” rule returns **four** `[101,202,303,606]`, whereas `[51,5151]` has two parts and is excluded by the cap. Divisor rules return **None** at 101, displayed as misleading `rule 0`. Scores use `table::TABLE`'s corrected 43/59/93. An independent Python `Fraction` enumeration reproduces 32/49 and 34/49 at cap 1000; raising only the cap to10000 changes the fewest-parts divisor score from 32 to 25, while the smallest-last score stays 34 ([independent-rules.txt](independent-rules.txt)). No held-out evidence tests which rule the scribe used. **Change:** print full bounds and tie-breaking, distinguish no candidate from numeric zero, identify normalized targets, report sensitivity to bounds, and frame 34/49 as descriptive agreement, not recovery of an historical algorithm. The explicit no-novelty disclaimer is appropriate; it does not supply a literature comparison.

13. **Medium — “Reports every [slip] that fits” is false, and first-fit tallies are not mechanism frequencies.** `slips::explain():309` returns immediately for checker hints; :345 returns immediately for a recovered part sign; `in_the_list():287` discards all alternatives if one needed value occurs elsewhere on the page. In No.63 four one-sign list repairs exist (reproduced in [detail.txt](detail.txt)), but only one is emitted with full page context. That preference is reasonable, but differs from the README's exhaustive-model claim. Physical sign confusion is also inferred from decimal-place changes, not tested from glyph shape. **Change:** retain alternatives with evidence ranks, or explicitly describe the filtering and priority. Restrict “no other pair more than twice” to emitted candidates under these filters and the checked subset, not every scribal confusion in the papyrus.

14. **Medium — Valid accepted numbers can silently produce inexact zero.** `rhind double '1000000000001` exits successfully with **0**, although the exact result is nonzero. `scribe::greedy():152` abandons a nonzero remainder above the denominator guard; it also has a fixed iteration limit. `express()` returns the incomplete expression as if exact. This was not observed for the existing corpus's expected values (additional harness [expressions.txt](expressions.txt) is empty), but it is an API/CLI correctness bug and contradicts exactness. **Change:** return an explicit unsupported/overflow error or an exact fallback; never return a partial expansion without signaling its residual. Test `express(v).value()==v` over accepted input ranges.

15. **Low — Coverage and source-reading language is broader than the actual evidence.** README opens with “Every figure” but the table of tenths has **zero** checked lines; quoted figures, auxiliary columns and known wrong dimensions/results are intentionally uncomputed. Counts include generated computations from translated prose as well as copied working. README:244 says every page of both editions was read; `sources/notes/table-2n-a.md` explicitly says plates were not read glyph by glyph except a few entries. The notes also contain stale capability claims (e.g. no land-strip conversion, while the parser implements it). **Change:** say “the encoded, checkable arithmetic” and list coverage by figure type; distinguish copied, restored, generated and unrepresented material. Describe the original reading procedure exactly and update stale notes. Do not present the presence of a green check as evidence that all figures on that source line were tested.
